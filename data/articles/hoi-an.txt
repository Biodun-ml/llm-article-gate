# Hội An Itinerary: Lanterns, Tailors and the Mỹ Sơn Ruins

Hội An in central Vietnam is small, and three days is the right amount of time.

Day 1: walk the Ancient Town in the morning before the day-trippers arrive from Đà Nẵng. One ticket covers five heritage houses; the Japanese Covered Bridge is the one to prioritise. Order clothes from a tailor on the first day so there is time for a second fitting.

Day 2: rent a bicycle and ride twenty minutes through rice fields to An Bàng Beach. In the evening, book a cooking class that starts with a basket boat ride through the coconut palms in Cẩm Thanh.

Day 3: get up at 5 for the Mỹ Sơn sanctuary, a group of Cham temples in a jungle valley about an hour inland. Arriving at opening time means cool air and almost no other visitors.

Every night the river through the old town fills with paper lanterns, and you can pay a boatman to float one for you.

Where to stay: a homestay between the old town and the beach gives you quiet nights and easy access to both.
