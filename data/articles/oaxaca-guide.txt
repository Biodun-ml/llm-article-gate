# Oaxaca City Guide: Markets, Mezcal and Mountain Ruins

Oaxaca, in southern Mexico, is the city I recommend to anyone who cares more about food than beaches.

Start at Mercado Benito Juárez, two blocks south of the zócalo, and work your way through the stalls selling chapulines, quesillo and hot chocolate ground to order. The smoke-filled meat hall in the neighbouring Mercado 20 de Noviembre is where you pick raw cuts and have them grilled in front of you.

The Templo de Santo Domingo de Guzmán is the most ornate church in the city, and the cultural museum in its former monastery is worth two hours.

Monte Albán sits on a flattened mountaintop twenty minutes from the centre. Take the first shuttle at 8:30 from the hotel on Mina street; by noon the stone plazas are scorching.

A full-day tour to Hierve el Agua combines the petrified waterfalls with a stop at a family mezcal distillery in Santiago Matatlán. Bring a swimsuit for the mineral pools at the top.

If you have an extra day, a cooking class in a village outside the city is the best way to learn the seven moles. Most include a market visit in the morning.

Where to stay: anywhere within walking distance of the zócalo. Getting there: there are daily flights from Mexico City, or a six-hour bus.
