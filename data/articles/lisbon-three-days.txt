# Three Days in Lisbon: A Practical Itinerary

Lisbon rewards slow walking, so this plan keeps each day to one or two neighbourhoods.

Day 1 starts in Alfama, the oldest quarter, where the lanes are too narrow for cars. Climb to the Miradouro de Santa Luzia early, before the tour groups arrive. In the evening, book a table at a small fado house in Alfama; most seat fewer than forty people and fill up by Thursday.

Day 2 is for Belém, a twenty-minute tram ride west along the river. The Jerónimos Monastery opens at 9:30 and the queue is shortest in the first hour. Afterwards, walk to the Belém Tower and pick up custard tarts from the bakery next to the monastery. Spend the late afternoon at LX Factory, a converted textile mill full of bookshops and cafés.

Day 3 is a day trip to Sintra, about forty minutes by train from Rossio station. Pena Palace is the one everyone photographs, but the Moorish Castle next door has better views and fewer people. Buy timed tickets online the day before.

Where to stay: Baixa is flat and central, which matters more than you think on Lisbon's hills. Getting around: buy a rechargeable Viva Viagem card at any metro station.
