# Six Months With a Hard-Shell Carry-On: Review

I bought this 40-litre hard-shell carry-on before a trip to Japan and have since used it on eleven flights, so this review is about how it holds up rather than how it looks out of the box.

The good: the polycarbonate shell has only light scuffs, the telescoping handle has no wobble, and the four spinner wheels still roll straight on airport floors. The clamshell layout with a zip divider keeps clothes on one side and shoes on the other.

The bad: at 3.4 kilograms empty it is heavy for its size, which matters on airlines with a 7-kilogram cabin limit. The built-in TSA lock is fiddly with cold fingers, and the front laptop pocket only fits a 14-inch machine.

Price: it is regularly discounted by around a fifth, so wait for a sale.

Verdict: a durable bag for people who mostly fly full-service carriers. If you fly budget airlines with strict weight limits, a soft-sided bag will serve you better.
